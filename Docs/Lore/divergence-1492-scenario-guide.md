### PRE-CONTACT: 1480-1492

**Player Activities:**
- Building military preparedness
- Strengthening alliances between civilizations
- Establishing coastal observation posts
- Deciding openness to Pacific trade
- Setting diplomatic protocols

**Intelligence Gathering:**
Through Pacific trade contacts (Chinese merchants), player receives information:
- "European kingdoms seek route to Asia"
- "Portuguese exploring African coast"
- "Spanish kingdoms recently united"
- "Europeans have gunpowder weapons, steel, horses" (player already has these via Pacific trade)

**Player Decision:**
**How prepared should Americas be when Europeans arrive?**

| Option | Military | Diplomacy | Economy | Result |
|--------|----------|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Militarized** | High coastal defenses, standing armies | Basic diplomatic corps | Trade focused on weapons | Ready for war, diplomacy harder |
| **Diplomatic** | Basic defenses, small armies | Large diplomatic corps, interpreters | Diverse trade networks | Ready to negotiate, vulnerable if fails |
| **Balanced** | Moderate defenses, professional armies | Diplomatic corps prepared | Balanced economy | Flexible response |
| **Isolationist** | Heavy defenses, large armies | Minimal diplomacy | Self-sufficient | Can defend but can't communicate well |
| **Unprepared** | Weak defenses | No diplomatic preparation | Vulnerable economy | Like real history - bad outcome |

**Key Mechanic:** **Preparedness Score**
- Military readiness: 0-100
- Diplomatic capability: 0-100
- Economic strength: 0-100
- Alliance unity: 0-100 (agreement among ten civilizations)

These scores determine options available during contact.

### FIRST CONTACT: October 12, 1492

**Scenario Intro:**
```
ALERT: Three unknown vessels spotted in Caribbean waters
Caribbean observation post reports: 
- Three ships, European design
- ~90 crew total
- Armed but not warship-scale
- Approaching Guanahani (San Salvador)

DECISION REQUIRED: How do you respond?
```

**Player Choices:**

#### OPTION 1: Immediate Military Response
**Requirements:** Military readiness 70+
**Description:** Intercept with war canoes. Surround vessels. Demonstrate strength.
**Outcome:**
- Europeans intimidated but not hostile
- Forced to port at major city
- No bloodshed
- **+Respect, -Trust**

#### OPTION 2: Diplomatic Welcome
**Requirements:** Diplomatic capability 60+
**Description:** Send diplomatic vessel with interpreters. Welcome them formally.
**Outcome:**
- Europeans surprised by sophistication
- Escorted to port at major city
- Friendly first impression
- **+Trust, -Respect** (they think you're naive)

#### OPTION 3: Observe First
**Requirements:** Intelligence network 50+
**Description:** Track them covertly. Learn before revealing presence.
**Outcome:**
- Gather information about intentions
- Can make informed decision later
- Delayed contact
- **Balanced response**

#### OPTION 4: Ignore Them
**Requirements:** None (always available)
**Description:** Let them explore. Don't engage yet.
**Outcome:**
- Europeans land, explore, take samples
- May establish small camp
- Contact inevitable later
- **Lost initiative** (they chose contact terms, not you)

**Historical Note:** 
In real history, Taino people welcomed Columbus friendly. He enslaved them.
In this timeline, player can choose different approach.

**Recommended:** Observe first if intelligence high, otherwise diplomatic welcome with military backup.

### THE FIRST MEETING: October 13-15, 1492

After initial contact, Europeans (Columbus's crew) meet American representatives.

**Scene Setup:**
```
Location: Port city in Caribbean (player chooses which civilization hosts)
Present: Columbus + senior crew (10 people)
         American diplomatic delegation (30+ people)
         Interpreters (who speak Chinese, hoping these are Chinese)
         Military escort (visible but not threatening)

Europeans expect: Primitive natives, easy gold
Europeans find: Stone buildings, organized government, armed guards
```

**The Conversation:**

**Phase 1: Establishing Communication**
- Player has interpreters who speak Chinese (expecting Asian traders)
- Europeans don't speak Chinese
- Eventually communicate through:
  - Trade pidgin (if Pacific trade was high)
  - Basic signs and drawings
  - Mathematical symbols (Maya and Europeans both understand numbers)

**Player Choice:** How much to reveal?
- **Full honesty:** "We are ten allied civilizations spanning two continents with population of 65 million"
- **Partial honesty:** "We are organized kingdoms in this region"
- **Deception:** "We are simple island people" (requires low military presence)
- **Intimidation:** Demonstrate military power immediately

**Phase 2: Intentions**
Columbus explains (through translation): "Seeking route to Asia for trade"

**Player Choice:** How to respond?
- **Helpful:** "Asia is west across great ocean. We trade with them. We can guide you."
- **Neutral:** "We know of Asia. This is not Asia. These are our lands."
- **Hostile:** "You are in our waters. State your purpose or leave."
- **Curious:** "Tell us of your homeland. We wish to learn."

**Phase 3: Trade Discussion**
Columbus wants: Gold, spices, route to Asia
Player has: Gold (plenty), spices (some), knowledge of Asia

**Player Choice:** What to offer?
- **Generous trade:** Offer gold, spices, information about Pacific routes
  - **Risk:** They think you're weak/exploitable
  - **Benefit:** Good relations start
- **Restricted trade:** Offer limited goods, no geographic information
  - **Balance:** They see you're cautious but willing to trade
- **No trade:** "We don't know you yet. Return with formal diplomatic mission."
  - **Benefit:** Show strength, don't give away leverage
  - **Risk:** They may try to take by force

**Historical Context:**
In real history, Taino gave gold freely. Spanish took more. Then enslaved them.
Player has information Spanish don't have: they come from powerful kingdoms with armies.

**Recommended:** Restricted trade. Show you have valuable goods but don't give everything away. Establish that trade requires formal agreements.

### COLUMBUS RETURNS TO SPAIN: November 1492 - March 1493

**Interlude:**
Columbus sails back to Spain with news.

**Player Receives Intel (via Pacific contacts):**
```
Chinese merchants report:
"The Europeans who visited you have returned to their homeland.
They report finding 'new islands' with 'gold and friendly natives.'
Their kingdoms are preparing larger expedition.
Estimated 1,000-1,500 soldiers, settlers, priests.
Expected arrival: Mid-1493."
```

**Player Decision: How to Prepare?**

**Mini-game: The Preparation Phase (4 months)**

**Resources to Allocate:**
- **Military:** Train troops, build fortifications, position forces
- **Diplomatic:** Prepare translators, draft treaties, establish protocols
- **Economic:** Stockpile trade goods, stabilize markets, prepare for disruption
- **Intelligence:** Monitor their approach, scout their capabilities
- **Alliance:** Coordinate with other American civilizations

**Key Mechanic:** **Resource Allocation Wheel**
Player has 100 preparation points to distribute.
Each category needs minimum 10 to be functional.
But spreading too thin = mediocrity in all areas.

**Strategic Choices:**
- **Heavy Military (70+):** Ready for war, but signals hostility
- **Heavy Diplomatic (70+):** Ready to negotiate, but vulnerable if they attack
- **Balanced (40/30/30):** Flexible but not excellent at any
- **Intelligence Focus (50+):** Know everything they're doing, can respond perfectly
- **Alliance Unity (50+):** All ten civilizations coordinate = massive advantage

**Historical Note:**
In real history, Spanish returned with 17 ships, 1,200 men, intent to colonize.
In this timeline, player has months to prepare for this.

**Recommended Strategy:**
1. Alliance Unity to 40 (get major civilizations on same page)
2. Intelligence to 30 (know when they're coming)
3. Military to 20 (basic defenses)
4. Diplomatic to 10 (can communicate)
5. Reserve 20 for emergency response when they arrive

### THE SECOND EXPEDITION: September 1493

**Arrival:**
```
ALERT: 17 European vessels approaching Caribbean
Intelligence report:
- ~1,200 soldiers
- Construction materials for fortification
- Settlers (men, women, children)
- Priests and religious items
- Agricultural supplies
- Large amount of weapons

Assessment: This is colonization force, not trading mission
```

**Player sees this on strategic map:**
- 17 ships (represented by icons)
- Heading toward Hispaniola (where Columbus made first contact)
- Can intercept, allow, or redirect

**Player Decision: CRITICAL CHOICE**

#### OPTION A: INTERCEPT AT SEA
**Requirements:** Military 60+, Naval capability
**Description:** Meet them in Caribbean waters with overwhelming naval force. Demand explanation before allowing landing.

**Outcome:**
Spanish admiral sees:
- 100+ war canoes/ships surrounding his fleet
- Armed warriors with steel weapons, muskets visible
- Organized military formation
- No fear

**Sub-choice:**
1. **Demand they leave:** "These waters are ours. You may not settle here."
   - If they refuse â†’ Naval battle (you have numbers, they have bigger ships)
   - If they agree â†’ Diplomatic victory (no war, but tense relations)

2. **Allow limited landing:** "You may establish one trading post. No settlements."
   - They accept â†’ Treaty of early 1493 (shortcut to diplomacy)
   - They refuse â†’ Naval battle

3. **Attack immediately:** "You come with army. This is invasion."
   - Naval battle begins
   - **No going back**

**Historical Parallel:**
Japanese response to Portuguese/Spanish in 1600s: "One trading post at Nagasaki. That's it. No missionaries. No military."
Worked because Japan was powerful enough to enforce it.

---

#### OPTION B: ALLOW LANDING, CONFRONT ON SHORE
**Requirements:** Military 40+
**Description:** Let them land and begin construction. Then arrive with overwhelming ground force.

**Outcome:**
Spanish begin building fort on Hispaniola (La Isabela, like real history).

Player arrives with:
- 5,000 warriors
- Steel weapons
- Muskets and cannon
- Cavalry
- Organized military formation

Spanish see:
- They're outnumbered 4-to-1
- Enemy has equivalent weapons
- Enemy has horses (wasn't expecting this)
- Enemy is organized and disciplined

**Sub-choice:**
1. **Demand they dismantle fort and leave**
   - If they refuse â†’ Battle of Santo Domingo
   - If they agree â†’ Diplomatic victory

2. **Allow fort but restrict activity**
   - "One fort. No expansion. Trade only. No enslaving locals."
   - They might accept (seeing they can't win war)

3. **Attack the fort**
   - Battle of Santo Domingo begins immediately
   - Higher casualties (fortified position)

---

#### OPTION C: NEGOTIATE BEFORE CONFLICT
**Requirements:** Diplomatic 60+
**Description:** Send diplomatic mission before they land. Negotiate settlement terms.

**Outcome:**
Diplomats board Spanish flagship.

Message delivered: 
"You have entered territory of the Caribbean Confederation, allied with nine other sovereign civilizations. We welcome trade. We do not permit unauthorized military settlement. Let us negotiate terms."

**Spanish Response Options:**
1. **Agree to negotiate** (if your military presence is visible)
   - Go directly to Treaty negotiations
   - Avoid war entirely
   - **Best outcome**

2. **Refuse, demand right to settle** (if they think you're weak)
   - Back to military options
   - But you tried diplomacy first

3. **Stall while landing forces** (if they're aggressive)
   - They're landing during negotiations
   - Player catches them
   - Trust destroyed, battle likely

**Historical Note:**
This didn't happen in real history because Taino weren't organized enough to project power.
In this timeline, you are. Big difference.

---

#### OPTION D: LET THEM SETTLE, THEN BESIEGE
**Requirements:** Patience, Military 50+
**Description:** Allow fort construction. Let them settle in. Then lay siege.

**Outcome:**
Spanish build La Isabela fort over several months.
Settlers arrive. They think they've succeeded.

Then:
- Supply lines are cut
- Fort is surrounded
- No escape
- Starve them out

**Advantages:**
- Lower casualties for you
- Demonstrates you're not weak, just patient
- They're in hopeless position

**Disadvantages:**
- Takes months
- Some settlers die (moral issue)
- Other European powers might see this and not try diplomacy

**Historical Parallel:**
Native American tribes did this successfully against some early forts.
Problem was Europeans kept sending more ships.
In this timeline, you can win the first battle decisively enough that they stop trying.

### THE BATTLE OF SANTO DOMINGO (If War Chosen)

**Setup:**
- Spanish: 1,200 soldiers, fortified position OR open field
- Caribbean Confederation: 5,000 warriors, home terrain advantage

**Forces Comparison:**

**Spanish Strengths:**
- Professional soldiers
- Steel armor
- Muskets (~200)
- Crossbows
- Cavalry (~50 horses)
- Cannon (~10)
- European tactical doctrine
- Fortification (if built)

**Caribbean Confederation Strengths:**
- Numbers (4-to-1 advantage)
- Steel weapons (via Pacific trade, 700 years practice)
- Muskets (~800, via Pacific trade, 600 years practice)
- Cavalry (~200 horses, via Pacific trade, 400 years experience)
- Cannon (~20, superior bronze work)
- Knowledge of terrain
- Supply lines (local)
- Motivation (defending homeland)

**The Battle (if fought):**

**Phase 1: Artillery Duel**
- Both sides have cannon
- You have more
- Spanish have better range (maybe)
- **Result:** Roughly equal, both take casualties

**Phase 2: Musket Exchange**
- Both sides have firearms
- You have more
- Spanish have better training (maybe)
- Cover and terrain matter
- **Result:** Slight advantage to you (numbers)

**Phase 3: Cavalry Clash**
- Both sides have horses
- You have more
- Spanish have better armor
- Your horses are acclimatized to Caribbean
- **Result:** Advantage to you (numbers + terrain)

**Phase 4: Melee**
- Both sides have steel weapons
- You have overwhelming numbers
- Spanish have better armor
- Close combat is bloody for both sides
- **Result:** You win through attrition

**Outcome:**
- **Spanish casualties:** 400-600 dead, 400 wounded, 200 captured
- **Your casualties:** 1,200-1,500 dead, 2,000 wounded
- **Spanish survivors:** Flee to ships
- **Result:** Decisive tactical victory

**Strategic Result:**
- Spanish expedition fails
- Survivors return to Spain with news: "They have armies and weapons"
- Spain reassesses: Can't conquer easily
- Opens door to Treaty negotiations

**Historical Note:**
Why didn't this happen in real timeline?
- Taino didn't have horses, steel, or gunpowder
- Taino weren't unified
- Disease killed 90% of population
In this timeline, all three differences are reversed.

### THE TREATY OF HAVANA: 1495

Whether reached through diplomacy or after military victory, negotiations begin.

**Participants:**
- Spanish Crown representatives
- Caribbean Confederation delegates
- Representatives from other American civilizations (observers/advisors)
- Mediators (possibly Chinese merchants from Pacific trade)

**Spanish Position:**
"We seek trade rights and limited settlement for trading posts."
(They wanted conquest, but now know that's not possible)

**Player Position:**
"We welcome trade. We do not welcome colonization. We are sovereign nations."

**Negotiation Minigame:**

**Issues to Resolve:**

1. **Trading Post Rights**
   - Where can Spanish establish posts?
   - How many?
   - What size?
   - Armed or unarmed?

2. **Territorial Boundaries**
   - Which islands are Spanish?
   - Which are Caribbean Confederation?
   - Can Spanish explore interior?

3. **Trade Terms**
   - What goods are traded?
   - At what prices?
   - Currency or barter?
   - Tariffs?

4. **Diplomatic Relations**
   - Are nations equal sovereigns?
   - Embassy exchange?
   - How are disputes resolved?

5. **Military Restrictions**
   - Ship limits in Caribbean waters?
   - Fort restrictions?
   - No privateering?

6. **Future European Contact**
   - Does treaty cover all European powers?
   - What happens when Portugal, England, France arrive?

**Player Choices (Each Issue):**

For each issue, player sees slider:
```

      â†‘
  (Move to set position)
```

**Mechanics:**
- Moving toward your favor: Spanish lose patience (may walk away)
- Moving toward their favor: You lose leverage (may be exploited later)
- Middle ground: Compromise (both somewhat satisfied)

**Overall Treaty Outcome:**

**Favorable Treaty (your favor 70+):**
- Limited Spanish presence
- Strong sovereignty
- Advantageous trade
- **But:** Spain might try again later, feeling cheated

**Balanced Treaty (40-60):**
- Reasonable for both sides
- Sustainable long-term
- **Best outcome:** Neither side feels exploited

**Unfavorable Treaty (your favor 30-):**
- Spanish get too much
- Sovereignty compromised
- **Risk:** They'll push for more
- **Risk:** Your own civilizations will be unhappy

**Historical Parallel:**
Treaties between European powers and Asian empires (Ottoman, Mughal, Chinese, Japanese) ranged from favorable to exploitative. Those that lasted were roughly balanced. Those that were one-sided eventually broke down.

**Recommended:** Aim for 50-50 balanced treaty. Sustainable peace is better than temporary advantage.

### AFTERMATH: 1496-1500

**If Treaty Succeeded:**
- Spanish trading posts established (3-5 locations)
- Trade begins: Spanish want gold, dyes, chocolate, medicinal plants
- Trade flows: You want European books, art, some technology
- Other European powers arrive (Portugal 1497, England 1498)
- Each must negotiate similar treaties
- **Precedent established:** Americas are equals, not colonies

**If War Continues:**
- Spanish send another expedition (smaller, more cautious)
- Cycle repeats until they accept they can't conquer
- Eventually leads to treaty anyway
- But more bloodshed, worse relations

**Long-term Impact:**
The 1492-1500 period establishes pattern for next 1000 years:
- Americas are trading partners, not colonies
- Knowledge flows both directions
- Both sides benefit from exchange
- No genocide, no slavery (in Americas; sadly continues in Africa)
- Parallel development rather than conquest

## ðŸŽ¯ KEY GAME DESIGN ELEMENTS

### Player Agency
Every outcome is result of player choices:
- Preparation level (1480-1492)
- Response to first contact (1492)
- Military vs. diplomatic balance (1493)
- Battle tactics (if war chosen)
- Treaty negotiation strategy (1495)

### Multiple Valid Paths
No "correct" playthrough:
- Pure diplomacy can work (if prepared)
- Pure military can work (if strong)
- Balanced approach is safest
- Even "failure" (unfavorable treaty) isn't game over

### Historical Education
Player learns:
- Why colonization succeeded in real history (tech gap + disease + disunity)
- How different it could be with different conditions
- How Asian empires resisted colonization (Japan, China, Siam)
- Real 1492 events and how they parallel/diverge

### Moral Complexity
No perfect choices:
- War means casualties on both sides
- Treaty means compromises
- Can't save everyone (African slavery continues)
- Good intentions don't guarantee good outcomes

### Replayability
Different approaches lead to different outcomes:
- Military playthrough: Win Battle of Santo Domingo decisively
- Diplomatic playthrough: Negotiate treaty without bloodshed
- Isolationist playthrough: Repel all European contact
- Open playthrough: Embrace exchange, maximum knowledge gain

Each playthrough explores different "what if" scenario.

## ðŸ’­ DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

**Core Question:**
"What does it take to resist colonization?"

**Answer the Game Provides:**
Not genetics. Not destiny. Not culture.
**Preparation. Unity. Technology. Information. Choices.**

**Why This Matters:**
Shows that history isn't inevitable.
Shows that colonization succeeded due to specific circumstances.
Shows that with different circumstances, outcomes could be radically different.

**Educational Value:**
Players learn more about real 1492 by playing alternate 1492.
By seeing what COULD prevent colonization, they understand what DID enable it.

**Emotional Impact:**
Native American players: "This is what could have been."
All players: "This is how contingent history is."

**Moral Lesson:**
Not "Americas were perfect and Europeans evil"
But "With preparation and unity, peoples can defend their sovereignty"
And "History is made by choices, not destiny"

## ðŸŽ¯ FINAL NOTES FOR IMPLEMENTATION

**Pacing:**
- Pre-contact phase: 15-20 minutes (building preparation)
- First contact: 5 minutes (dramatic encounter)
- Second expedition: 30-45 minutes (major decision-making)
- Battle (if chosen): 20-30 minutes (tactical gameplay)
- Treaty negotiation: 20-30 minutes (diplomatic gameplay)
- **Total scenario time: 90-120 minutes**

**Difficulty Modes:**
- **Story Mode:** Focuses on narrative, easier battles/negotiations
- **Historical Mode:** Realistic difficulty, shows how hard resistance really is
- **Challenge Mode:** Intentionally handicapped start (low preparation), must overcome
- **Sandbox Mode:** No fixed outcomes, experiment with different scenarios

**Accessibility:**
- Colorblind mode (important for map reading)
- Text size options (lots of reading in treaty)
- Audio descriptions (for visually impaired)
- Simplified controls option (for younger players)
- Skip battle option (focus on diplomacy)
- Skip diplomacy option (focus on battles)

**Modding Support:**
- Let players adjust starting conditions
- Create alternative first contact scenarios (Portuguese first? Chinese first? Multiple nations at once?)
- Historical accuracy vs. game balance sliders
- Custom treaty terms
- Different European strategies
